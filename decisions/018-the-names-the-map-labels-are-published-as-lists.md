# 018 — The names the map labels are published as lists a site can search

**Status:** accepted; amended the same day, twice on 2026-10-19 and on 2026-10-22 · **Decided:** 2026-10-16, by Peter

## Context

SalishSea.io will let people search for a place by name and go to it on the map. The names it needs are on the basemap, but only as labels inside vector tiles, which a browser draws and can't search. Those names come from two places. The names of the water and of points on it come from the two national gazetteers, placed by [scripts/names.py](../scripts/names.py) ([decision 006](006-marine-names-and-the-boundary-come-from-government-gazetteers.md)). Islands, towns and ferry terminals come from OpenStreetMap, through Protomaps' tiles.

## Decision

**Each version publishes the names the map labels as two lists, kept apart because their licences differ** ([scripts/places.py](../scripts/places.py)):

- `places-gazetteers.json` holds the water bodies and points that `names.py` chose, at the positions and from the zooms the map labels them. US names are in the public domain, and Canadian names are under the Open Government Licence – Canada.
- `places-osm.json` holds islands, cities, towns, villages and ferry terminals, from OpenStreetMap, under the ODbL.

**Only kinds the map labels are listed.** Peter chose this over two wider lists. One would have added everything in the gazetteers near salt water, such as beaches, rocks and summits. The other would have held every name in the region.

**Amended: only the Salish Sea's places, and only the larger ones** (Peter, the same day). As first published in 2026.10.12, the lists held every name the map labels across the coast: 17,725 places, 272 KB gzipped, ten times SalishSea.io's whole search index. Peter's point was that people search for hundreds of places, not tens of thousands. So the lists now hold only places inside SalishSea.io's Salish Sea region (126–122°W, 47–50.5°N), and of those ([scripts/places.py](../scripts/places.py), `searched`):

- water bodies and points the map labels by zoom 12, which leaves out most coves and the points inside them;
- islands that fit the map at zoom 13 or closer, from Hat Island up, which leaves out islets and rocks;
- cities, towns and villages, and no hamlets, suburbs or neighbourhoods;
- one ferry terminal for each landing the map's ferry routes reach. OpenStreetMap tags each berth and foot-passenger ramp as a terminal, and some port berths too, such as "US Oil #1" and "Pier 7-A" in Tacoma. So a terminal more than 1 km from a route is left out, terminals within 800 m of one another are one landing, and the landing takes the name whose words most of its records share: "Swartz Bay Ferry Terminal", not "Seaspan Ferries" or "Victoria (Swartz Bay) Berth 1" ([scripts/places.py](../scripts/places.py), `landings`). That turns 218 records into 92 landings.

That is about 2,100 places, 31 KB gzipped. Of the 293 place names that appear at least twice in the locations of 3,976 sighting reports Peter has gathered, these rules keep 70%, which is 84% of the mentions. They lose Bella Bella, on the central coast, beaches such as Richmond Beach, which the lists never held, and small islands such as Cutts Island. Rules this simple can't reach a few hundred without losing places people name often: kept to zoom 11 and islands at zoom 12, the lists hold about 1,000 places but lose Hat Island, Lagoon Point, Dash Point and Hood Canal.

**The map now labels islets and small islands.** Protomaps files them under its points of interest, as the kind `coastline`, and its style never draws them, so a search for Coon Island in the Wasp Islands would have gone to an unlabelled rock. They are drawn as Protomaps draws other islands, in grey italic, from the zoom Protomaps gives each, which is 15 for most ([style/style.ts](../style/style.ts), `small_island_labels`). Peter chose this over leaving them out of the list: rocks and islets are how people say where whales were, and at that zoom there is room for their names. Since the amendment above, the lists leave them out after all, but the map still labels them. The image tiles have no labels and are unchanged. (Amended 2026-10-19, by Peter: Protomaps keeps an islet only when it is also tagged `natural=coastline`, so many tagged only `place=islet` were never labelled, such as Ben Ure, Deception, Pass and Northwest Islands at Deception Pass, where only Strawberry Island had a name. [scripts/islets.py](../scripts/islets.py) now names every named islet, and every island OpenStreetMap draws only as a point, from OpenStreetMap, at the point inside it furthest from the shore, two zooms before the islet fits one tile, from 11 to 15. The names ride in the landmarks' archive, also OpenStreetMap's and also only labels, and are drawn as the islands are, above Protomaps' small-island points, so where both name one islet, only one name shows. Over the test window that is 267 names.) (Amended 2026-10-22, by Peter: the script first also named islands too small to fit one tile at zoom 12, on the belief that Protomaps left them unlabelled. It doesn't: Protomaps labels every island OpenStreetMap outlines, however small, so those names were drawn twice from about zoom 13, as Ketron Island's was. Each of the 171 names this dropped over the test window was checked against Protomaps' tiles; Protomaps labels every one, from the same zoom or earlier for 139 and a zoom later for 30, among them Eliza, Matia and Patos Islands.)

**Each place gives a position to look at and a zoom to look from.** For a water body or a point, that is where the map writes its label and the zoom from which it does. For an island drawn as an outline, it is the middle of the outline and the zoom at which the whole island fills about one 512-pixel tile. A group of islands that OpenStreetMap outlines one by one under the group's name, such as the Trail Islands, is listed once, shown whole. Zooms are MapLibre's, which count 512-pixel tiles. For a town or a ferry terminal, it is OpenStreetMap's point, at a zoom set by its kind: 11 for a city, 12 for a town, 13 for a village and 15 for a ferry terminal. Other names that OpenStreetMap records, such as `alt_name`, `short_name` and `official_name`, go with the place, so that a search for Salt Spring Island finds Saltspring Island.

**Amended 2026-10-19: the OpenStreetMap list says which island a namesake is on** (Peter). SalishSea.io's search told two places of one name apart by the island or town whose middle was nearest, which is often the wrong island for a place on a shore: the Cattle Point on San Juan Island's southern tip was "near Lopez Island", whose middle is 8 km from it against San Juan Island's 12. Only the islands' outlines can say which island a place is on, and they come from OpenStreetMap. So the island goes in the OpenStreetMap list, under the ODbL, and not in the gazetteers' list, whose licences it would otherwise change. It is a table under `gazetteer_islands`, with its own `columns` (`name`, `lon`, `lat`, `island`): each gazetteer place that shares its name with another of its kind, by its name and position, and the listed island whose outline is nearest it, within 300 m for a point, which sits on the shore or just inland, and 1 km for a water body, whose label is moved out into the water ([scripts/places.py](../scripts/places.py), `islands_of`). In the 2026.10.13 lists that is 90 of the 152 such places. Only those, because telling them apart is what the table is for, and the island of every place would have taken the OpenStreetMap list from about 10 KB gzipped to 25 KB; these add about 1.5 KB. A site joins the two lists by name and position, which the build writes the same way in both.

**Each list is one JSON object.** It says what the list holds, its sources, its licence and the attribution it needs, then names its columns (`name`, `kind`, `lon`, `lat`, `zoom`, `other_names`) and gives its places as rows, which keeps the file small. Over the Salish Sea, the two lists hold about 2,100 places and are about 31 KB gzipped.

**A version's style names the lists in its metadata, under `salishsea:places`, by name (`gazetteers` and `osm`),** as it names its image tiles ([decision 014](014-image-tiles-are-drawn-from-the-style-along-the-shore.md)). Like the archives, each list is stored once under a name made from its contents (`places/places-osm-<hash>.json`), so a version that doesn't change the names shares the earlier version's lists.

**No list of aliases.** Sighting reports shorten names, such as EDM for Edmonds. Peter's view is that the abbreviations keep reports brief and that people don't expect them to work in a search field. A shortened name, such as "edm" or "lime point", should find its place because each word starts a word of the name, and that is for the search to do, not the list.

## Rejected

- **Everything near salt water**, adding the gazetteers' beaches, rocks, reefs, bars and summits. Many of these places have no label on the map, so a search would go to an unmarked spot.
- **Every name in the region.** About half of the gazetteers' names around the Salish Sea are creeks, lakes, swamps and glaciers. In Washington alone, OpenStreetMap names over 5,000 parks and 3,000 peaks.
- **Reading the names out of the tiles.** A label is repeated in every tile it touches and is cut at the tiles' zooms, so the list would have to be rebuilt from the pieces. The sources the tiles were made from are already in the build.
- **One list.** The OpenStreetMap names are under the ODbL, which this project keeps apart from the public-domain and open-government data ([README](../README.md#what-it-is-built-from)).
- **An `island` column in the gazetteers' list.** The island is found from OpenStreetMap's outlines, so the column would bring that list under the ODbL.
- **Islands found from the depth grids,** as land above sea level named by the gazetteers' own island points, which would have kept the island out of OpenStreetMap's terms. More work, and at 30 m, islands joined by a spit or a flat that dries would be one.
- **Orcapelago's hand-kept list of aliases.** It is not ours to publish, and per Peter its abbreviations aren't search terms.
- **Every name the map labels, across the coast**, as 2026.10.12 published them. Too heavy, and nearly all of it names places nobody searches for.
- **The places sighting reports name**, chosen by counting which names reports use. That fits what people search for best, but a place nobody has reported from would not be findable, and the choice would move with the reports rather than with the map. A small set chosen by rules, with places from reports added, was also turned down.
- **A hand-kept list** of a few hundred places. Exactly the places wanted, but kept by hand.

## Known problems

- Many places share a name, such as the several Mud Bays. The OpenStreetMap list names the island each is on (amended above), but a place on the mainland, or on an island as big as Vancouver Island or Whidbey Island, has none that tells it apart, and a site must name a town instead.
- The zoom for a town or a ferry terminal is set by its kind, not by the zoom at which Protomaps first draws its label, which also depends on population.
- OpenStreetMap's islands include islands in lakes and rivers.
- A place is kept if its middle is inside the Salish Sea region, so an island that straddles the region's edge is listed or not by where its middle falls. Bella Bella, Tofino and the rest of the coast outside the region are not listed, though SalishSea.io shows sightings there.
- Ferry landings within 800 m of one another are one, so the two ends of a short crossing are listed once: the Barnston Island ferry's landings are "Port Kells", and Newcastle Island's dock is merged with Protection Island's. Victoria's harbour ferry stops and False Creek's are one landing each.
- Beaches are not listed, though reports often name them (Richmond Beach, Gold Beach), and the smallest islands and the coves are left out.
- A name the reports use that is not the official one is not listed. Reports' "Jeff Head" appears to be GNIS's Point Jefferson: a road out to it is Jefferson Point Road, and the reports that name it place whales off it. But neither GNIS nor OpenStreetMap records "Jefferson Head", not even among GNIS's variant names, so adding those would not find it. SalishSea.io's `salish-3zok.21` carries what to do, once its search log (its decision 078) shows which names people look for.
- Parks, beaches, peaks and the landmarks ([decision 015](015-landmarks-are-what-the-charts-mark-and-tall-towers.md)) are labelled on the map but not listed.
